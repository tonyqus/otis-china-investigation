## 2. Wuhan bank procurement, 2012

The SEC order describes four elevators for a state-owned bank branch in Wuhan. A bank official requested a kickback; the Otis Wuhan sales supervisor agreed and arranged distributor bidding, falsely attributing the need for a distributor to unacceptable bank terms. Review did not challenge the justification or pricing spread. An unauthorized distributor was introduced using a fake chop. The supervisor received USD 98,000, paid part to the bank official and retained the rest.

Paragraph 38 records that UTC learned of this conduct after Chinese authorities convicted the supervisor. The order does not disclose the bank, supervisor, distributors, Chinese judgment number, sentence or exact Chinese statutory offense. “Bribery” must not be converted into an assumed Chinese charge. The funds transferred to the supervisor are not necessarily all the bank official's bribe.

Outstanding: Chinese judgment, identities, precise offense/sentence, distributor legal entities and full allocation. See [CN-001](../cases/CN-001-wuhan-bank/case-summary.en.md), E001.

## 3. Hangzhou Metro Line 1

SEC paragraph 41 describes a USD 27.6 million contract obtained in 2008. In 2010 a manager approved a 2011 Italy/Greece trip for seven project-related officials, ostensibly to inspect subway equipment. The inspections did not occur and the manager knew the trip was for leisure. This regulatory finding is not a Chinese criminal conviction. See [CN-002](../cases/CN-002-hangzhou-metro/case-summary.en.md), E001.

## 4. Shenzhen Metro Line 5

SEC paragraph 42 describes a USD 3.3 million contract obtained in 2008 and a 2011 US trip for seven officials, nominally to inspect New York subways and Otis facilities. Inspections did not occur; visits included New York and Washington, DC. Otis China spent USD 37,926. Chinese administrative/criminal follow-up is unverified. See [CN-003](../cases/CN-003-shenzhen-metro/case-summary.en.md), E001.

## 5. Yiwu Central Hospital

2011 reporting quotes Jinhua Intermediate People's Court case **（2003）金中刑二终字第70号**. It describes Zhejiang Zhe'ao Elevator Sales Co., Ltd. salesperson Zhang paying RMB 90,000 to evaluator Wang in connection with Guangzhou Otis products. This is a distributor employee allegation/history; the purchased brand alone does not establish manufacturer participation or knowledge.

The reported sequence is eight shortlisted bidders in August 2000, Zhe'ao and Xizi Otis preliminarily selected August 23, Wang favoring Guangzhou Otis in September, contract November 8, deposit RMB 1.028 million November 15 and payment to Wang days later. The account says Wang returned the RMB 90,000 and Zhang's remuneration depended on sales collections. These remain journalistic quotations, not facts independently read in the judgment original.

Obtain the complete judgment, appellate disposition, offense/sentence and manufacturer–distributor relationship. See [CN-004](../cases/CN-004-yiwu-hospital/case-summary.en.md), E003/E004/E009.

## 6. Hunan adverse commercial-bribery list, 2008

2011 reporting cites a July 2008 Changsha Evening News report on 39 companies listed by the Hunan Health Department, including Hangzhou Xizi Otis. The reported restriction prohibited public healthcare institutions from purchasing listed products for two years from August 1, 2008.

The notice is identified as **湘卫办发〔2008〕14号**. A July 18, 2008 A5 newspaper archive has a relevant title; full-page access was paywalled. A July 23 republication allegedly contains the list, but direct access returned a loading page. Search excerpts show a malformed company name, “杭州西子奥的斯限电梯有限公司”; that is not an independently verified official entity entry.

A 2009 Hunan government document-cleanup catalogue lists the underlying policy **湘卫办发〔2007〕35号**. A policy catalogue proves neither an individual listing nor its grounds. Obtain notice 14 and the complete attachment, specific conduct, amounts and legal basis. See [CN-005](../cases/CN-005-hunan-list/case-summary.en.md), E005–E009.

## 7. Shanghai Jin Yun

Government-hosted Newmin reporting on November 6, 2014, a district news-center article November 12 and Shangguan reporting December 15 describe one matter, not three cases. Reports say sales supervisor Jin Yun helped prepare different-value contracts in 1996; a trade intermediary retained a USD 160,000 difference and paid him USD 20,000.

Police investigated in 2000. Reports say he returned USD 20,000, was released pending proceedings, left China and was apprehended in Shanghai on August 18, 2014. No indictment, final judgment or sentence has been obtained. A government website hosting news is not a court decision. Do not infer corporate instructions or sum the two amounts as separate losses. The reported RMB conversion has not been adopted without checking.

Outstanding: Chinese charging/judgment records, offense, sentence, legal entities, purchasing mall, contracts and fund records. Overseas flight does not turn the underlying Chinese transaction into overseas procurement. See [CN-006](../cases/CN-006-shanghai-jin-yun/case-summary.en.md), E010–E012.

## 8. SEC document and entity boundaries

Wuhan, Hangzhou and Shenzhen are distinct matters in one SEC administrative order concerning the then parent UTC. Global settlement sums must not be attributed wholly to China. Other-country projects and UTC aviation businesses are not Otis China elevator cases.

The order says UTC settled without admitting or denying findings apart from jurisdiction; its page 2 footnote says findings are not binding on other persons/entities in other proceedings. Say “the SEC records a Chinese conviction,” not “we obtained the Chinese judgment.”

Historical entities include Otis Elevator (China), its branches, Hangzhou Xizi Otis / Xizi Otis, Otis Electric Elevator and independent distributors or contractors. Historical names, brand or commercial relationships alone do not transfer liability. Check the legal entity, contract, payer and participants at the relevant time.

## 9. Historical mechanisms

The documented/reported mechanisms include distributor price spreads and kickbacks, purported equipment inspections that were leisure travel, and distinctions between distributor and manufacturer responsibility. They are not proof of current misconduct or misconduct on another project.

## 10. 2011 undercover reporting

National Business Daily's October 18 report describes salespeople discussing direct sales, agency commissions, resale spreads, inflated installation contracts and cover bids. Hypothetical examples include RMB 800,000 supply / RMB 1 million resale and increasing RMB 50,000 installation to RMB 60,000. They do not prove particular payments, a formal policy or judicial findings.

- [October 18 article](https://m.nbd.com.cn/articles/2011-10-18/608236.html)
- [October 19 supplier interviews](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2011-10-19/608475.html): conflicting comments on procurement kickbacks and controls are not a finding of actual receipt.

## 11. Shanghai civil distributor-reward dispute, not a bribery case

Shanghai No. 1 Intermediate People's Court **（2013）沪一中民四（商）终字第1982号**, January 6, 2014, concerned Shanghai Xincheng Elevator Engineering and Xizi Otis/Shanghai branch.

| Item | Recorded amount/data | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| August 22, 2006 bank payment labeled commission | RMB 26,200 | Xizi Otis described first-half volume reward |
| March 30, 2007 payment labeled business fee | RMB 30,000 | Xizi Otis described 2006 award |
| Total | RMB 56,200 | Not automatically bribery |
| Sales target, party statement | 100 units | Not a court-published commission formula |
| First-half signed/executed, party statement | 78 / 21 units | Does not establish per-unit reward |
| Installation subcontract amount | RMB 1,547,616 for reportedly 78 units | Not final settlement for 21 units |

RMB 26,200 / 78 is approximately RMB 336 per unit, an arithmetic average only. Xizi Otis asserted tourism services; unilateral participant lists were not accepted, so tourism must not be described as a fact found by the court.

## 13. 2019–2026 and unresolved leads

The latest substantive search, November 13, 2026, did not independently confirm a post-2018 newly occurring Otis China bribery matter through a Chinese criminal judgment, commercial-bribery decision or US enforcement document concerning China. This is a search result, not proof of absence. Later reporting on historical conduct is not new conduct. Maintenance penalties are separate.

- **Li Baojin / Tianjin Otis:** 2007 Caijing reporting of indictment allegations mentions Tianjin Otis in a benefit-arranging sequence for Liu Ye. The company's precise role is unclear; no Otis bribery finding or allocation of whole-case sums follows. E013.
- **Ding Jinsong / Jinding:** 2016 reporting describes a unit-bribery conviction and 1999 Huangshan elevator procurement favored by officials, but names only a Hangzhou supplier. A biography's Xizi Otis dealership reference does not establish the contract's brand or link property-development bribery sums to elevators. E014.
- **Wenzhou 2019:** two unnamed maintenance firms reportedly suspected of bribery; Otis Electric Wenzhou appears in a different non-compliant-maintenance list. No identity link established. E015.
- **Northern-region/Changchun online allegations:** search snippets, not recovered article originals. Column pages returned 502/404 in earlier research. Dates, authorship, underlying attachments, identities, responses and proceedings unknown. Multiple snippets do not equal corroboration. Not counted. - **Jiangxi Youmai:** April 13, 2019 The Paper reporting quotes an August 3, 2018 Qingshanhu first-instance judgment: unit bribery, RMB 100,000 company fine; Zhong Bo, eight months' imprisonment suspended for one year. The reported scheme reduced inspection fees through changed contract values. Otis connection is limited to alleged seal-expense records; the manager denied seal forgery. No Otis authorization, participation or ownership established. Judgment original, number and finality missing. E018.

## 15. Outstanding primary records and source access

Highest priorities are Wuhan and Jin Yun judgments, Yiwu **（2003）金中刑二终字第70号**, and Hunan **湘卫办发〔2008〕14号** with its 39-company attachment. Then seek metro follow-up and additional cases using distributor names, purchasers and court identifiers.

Lawful options include requesting Hunan records from the provincial health commission and asking Jinhua court/archive about access. The SEC paragraph is a search lead for Wuhan, not assurance of access to non-public criminal files. Historic searches combined brand/entity names, relevant people, bribery/embezzlement terms and targeted court/procuratorate sites; they were not exhaustive judicial-database searches.
